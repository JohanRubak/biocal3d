2.4 Data exploration and scanner characteristics

The approved regions of interest were explored to characterise differences in colour, mesh sampling and surface topography between scanners and experimental stages. Fresh and mature specimens were analysed separately. Between-scanner comparisons paired scans of the same specimen at the same stage, while stage comparisons paired scans within each specimen and scanner.

Colour distributions were characterised in CIELAB space [R1] using the saved unlit RGB projections, assuming sRGB encoding [R2], D65 illumination and a 2° standard observer. Background pixels were excluded, and measurements were weighted by the corresponding visible surface area. Per-scan summaries included the mean, standard deviation, median and distribution percentiles of L*, a* and b*. Histograms were normalised within scans before averaging, giving specimens equal weight within each scanner–stage comparison.

Paired colour differences between scanners were quantified using CIEDE2000 (ΔE₀₀) between scan-mean CIELAB vectors [R3,R4], supplemented by one-dimensional Wasserstein distances between the marginal L*, a* and b* histograms [R5]. These measurements described exported colour appearance without an independent colour reference. Within-specimen colour changes were assessed using ΔE₀₀ and signed changes in mean L*, a* and b*. Baseline-to-clean comparisons were performed for both cohorts, with additional baseline-to-partial and partial-to-clean comparisons for mature specimens. Individual trajectories illustrated colour changes relative to each specimen’s baseline (Fig. 5). Colour conversion and CIEDE2000 calculations were implemented in scikit-image version 0.26.0 [R7].

Mesh sampling was characterised by vertex and face counts, counts per unit mesh surface area, triangle areas and geometric edge lengths. Encoded vertex counts were distinguished from counts of unique geometric vertices to account for duplicated coordinates. Surface topography was assessed relative to a fitted plane using mean absolute height, root-mean-square (RMS) height, the 5th–95th percentile height range and native face slopes. Native RMS height was calculated by triangle-area integration. Height measurements were additionally calculated after surface-area-weighted averaging into grids with cell widths of 50, 100 and 200 µm. Cells required at least 75% projected coverage, and missing regions were not filled. Triangle density, median native face slope and RMS height at 100 µm were selected for the principal geometric comparison (Fig. 6).

Matched visual comparisons used unlit colour projections, neutral-shaded native surfaces, native mesh edges and detrended height maps. BCG3-1 after partial cleaning illustrated differences between scanners (Fig. 7). Displays used a common 13.4 × 13.4 mm field of view, with a central 4 × 4 mm mesh zoom. Neutral geometry views used identical illumination and 20-fold vertical exaggeration for display only. Height maps used shared colour limits. Comparisons did not assume pointwise correspondence between scanner meshes.

A sixth, annotation-based analysis is planned to assess plaque–clean separability using colour and geometric features for each scanner. Feature distributions in annotated plaque-covered and exposed regions will be compared, prioritising partially cleaned specimens in which both regions coexist. Comparisons will account for specimen pairing and examine whether geometric features provide contrast where colour differences are limited.

Descriptive summaries used specimens as the independent units and reported medians and interquartile ranges. Selected median estimates were accompanied by 95% percentile confidence intervals obtained from 1,000 specimen-level bootstrap resamples [R6]. Full-cohort exploration described dataset characteristics. Data-dependent preprocessing parameters and model-design choices will be selected using training specimens before evaluation on held-out data.
