## 2.4 Data exploration and scanner characteristics

The manually approved regions of interest were explored to characterise scanner-dependent colour and geometric representations and changes associated with experimental stage. The analysis included 512 scans from 48 independent specimens. Fresh and mature specimens were analysed separately because their cleaning protocols contained two and three stages, respectively. Comparisons between scanners paired scans of the same specimen at the same stage, whereas comparisons between stages paired scans within each specimen and scanner.

Colour was characterised using CIELAB distributions obtained from the saved unlit RGB projections, assuming sRGB encoding, D65 illumination and a 2° standard observer. Background pixels were excluded, and pixel contributions were weighted by their corresponding surface area. Per-scan summaries included the mean, standard deviation and distribution percentiles of L*, a* and b*. Histograms were normalised within scans before aggregation so that scans contributed equally to group-level distributions. Between-scanner colour differences were quantified using CIEDE2000 (ΔE00) between the area-weighted mean CIELAB vectors of paired scans, supplemented by distances between their marginal colour distributions. These measurements described differences in exported appearance rather than colour accuracy against an independent reference.

Stage-related colour changes were assessed using paired differences in L*, a* and b* and ΔE00 between baseline and clean scans. Baseline-to-partial and partial-to-clean changes were additionally evaluated for mature specimens. These comparisons investigated whether the appearance changes associated with cleaning were represented similarly across scanners without assuming that a whole-specimen colour change directly measured plaque coverage.

Mesh sampling was characterised by vertex and face counts, counts per unit mesh surface area, triangle areas and geometric edge lengths. Encoded vertex counts were distinguished from counts of unique geometric vertices to account for duplicated coordinates. Surface topography was characterised relative to a fitted plane using mean absolute height, root-mean-square height, the 5th–95th percentile height range and face slopes. Height measurements were also calculated after surface-area-weighted averaging into common grids with cell widths of 50, 100 and 200 µm; cells required at least 75% projected coverage, and missing areas were not filled. These comparisons assessed dependence on the calculation scale without equalising the resolving power of the scanners. The measurements describe unfiltered scanner-derived topography rather than independently measured material roughness.

Visual comparisons used matched specimens displayed as unlit colour projections, neutral-shaded topography and detrended height maps. Specimen BCG6-4 illustrated scanner differences after partial cleaning and changes across all three experimental stages. Matched displays used common physical fields of view, shared height colour limits and identical shading settings. Scanner-specific image frames were not assumed to provide pointwise correspondence between scanners.

Once plaque annotations are available, a sixth analysis will assess plaque–clean separability in colour and geometric features for each scanner. Annotated plaque-covered and exposed regions will be compared, prioritising partially cleaned specimens in which both regions coexist. This analysis will test whether features with limited colour contrast show greater geometric contrast, particularly in LABscanner data, and will distinguish qualitative visibility from quantitative feature separation.

Descriptive summaries used the specimen as the independent unit and reported medians and interquartile ranges; confidence intervals for selected paired summaries were obtained using 1,000 specimen-level bootstrap resamples. The present exploration used the full dataset descriptively. Any subsequent data-dependent choices affecting preprocessing, feature selection or model design will be checked using training data before evaluation on held-out specimens.

## Colour differences before calibration

The uncalibrated scans showed scanner-dependent colour characteristics, including differences that persisted after cleaning. Among mature specimens, median paired ΔE00 between scan-mean colours ranged from 4.59 to 15.34 at baseline and from 4.84 to 11.39 after cleaning. The colour response to cleaning also differed between scanners: median baseline-to-clean ΔE00 was 2.94 for fresh specimens and 2.93 for mature specimens with the LABscanner, compared with 6.12–8.70 and 6.88–9.34, respectively, for the intraoral scanners. Median paired lightness changes were small for the LABscanner (ΔL* = +0.53 and +0.06), whereas all three intraoral scanners showed increases (+4.91 to +7.88). These differences motivate investigation of colour harmonisation. No calibration correction was evaluated in this analysis, and the reported ΔE00 values describe differences between exported representations rather than accuracy against an independent colour reference.
